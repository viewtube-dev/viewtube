## 1. How to use this pack

The four briefs are deliberately different:

- **ERA:** accelerator fit, founder-market learning, customer discovery and disciplined validation.
- **Forum Ventures:** B2B SaaS positioning, buyer definition, repeatable workflow pain and go-to-market validation.
- **Google Cloud:** technical architecture, bounded AI/media workload validation, cost and responsible data handling.
- **Betaworks:** product thesis, distinctive creator workflow experience, fast experimentation and community/product feedback.

These are working hypotheses about the best conversation angle, not verified statements about each organization's current investment thesis, program terms, application process or preferred stage. Before outreach, check official current materials and identify a named recipient whose remit matches the ask.

## 2. Shared positioning and claim controls

### Core company description

**ViewTube is building a connected workspace for professional video creators and teams, linking creator research, production planning, media assets, AI-assisted workflows, publishing operations and performance learning.**

The initial commercial wedge to test is **AI-assisted media asset management and video intelligence for professional creators and creator teams**. The broader platform thesis is that creators and teams lose time and context as work moves between disconnected tools. Both the wedge and the scale of the problem require customer validation.

### Short spoken pitch

Professional creators and media teams work across a fragmented stack: research in one place, production plans in another, media assets somewhere else, editing and publishing elsewhere, and performance learning in separate analytics tools. ViewTube is building a connected creator workspace that links those steps through shared projects, media context, AI-assisted workflows and analytics. We are validating the strongest initial use case with creators and teams, beginning with media asset management and video intelligence.

### What we are asking partners to help validate

1. Which creator or media-team segment has the most urgent, frequent and budgeted problem?
2. Which single workflow should become the first repeatable paid use case?
3. What evidence would demonstrate that ViewTube reduces friction or improves an operational outcome?
4. Which technical, distribution or ecosystem resources can accelerate that validation?
5. What should be deferred until demand, economics and implementation evidence justify it?

### Claims that must not appear without dated evidence

- User, creator, customer, pilot or paying-account counts.
- Revenue, ARR, growth, retention, conversion, churn, CAC or LTV.
- Time saved, cost reduction, throughput, reliability, latency or model accuracy.
- Retention uplift, view growth, revenue uplift or predictive performance.
- Production deployment on Google Cloud, Vertex AI, Gemini, AWS, Azure or NVIDIA infrastructure.
- Signed customers, pilots, partnerships, endorsements or program acceptance.
- Current partner eligibility, credit amounts, application windows or benefits.

## 3. Partner brief: Entrepreneurs Roundtable Accelerator (ERA)

### Strategic angle

**Build a focused creator-workflow company through disciplined customer discovery, not a broad feature-count pitch.**

The best initial conversation should test whether ViewTube can turn its broad connected-workflow vision into a narrow, urgent problem for a clearly defined buyer. The pitch should emphasize founder learning velocity, a demonstrable product, a plausible initial wedge and willingness to change direction based on customer evidence.

**Fit hypothesis:** ERA may be relevant if its current program, stage, geography and investment model align with the company's actual status. Verify current criteria and the appropriate application or introduction route before sending.

### Tailored pitch

ViewTube is building a connected workspace for professional video creators and teams. The product thesis is that valuable work gets lost between research, planning, media assets, production, publishing and performance learning. Rather than asking creators to adopt another isolated tool, ViewTube aims to connect the context and handoffs across that workflow.

We are testing a narrower entry point: AI-assisted media asset management and video intelligence for professional creators and creator teams. The immediate challenge is identifying the buyer with the strongest pain and willingness to pay, then proving one repeatable workflow before expanding the platform.

We are looking for an accelerator environment that can pressure-test the customer, sharpen the initial wedge, improve founder-led discovery and help turn a technically broad product into a focused, measurable business.

### Best first ask

A short fit conversation or application review focused on:
- Whether the company's current stage and founder profile match the current program.
- Whether the creator/vertical SaaS thesis fits the current investment and mentor network.
- What evidence ERA would expect before accepting or advancing an application.
- Whether a small set of targeted founder/customer introductions is appropriate.

Do not lead with a request for capital alone. Lead with a clear problem, a live demonstration and specific learning objectives.

### 90-second meeting version

We are building ViewTube for professional video creators and teams whose work is fragmented across research, planning, assets, production, publishing and analytics. The long-term vision is a connected workflow, but we know that breadth is not itself a go-to-market strategy. We are narrowing the first wedge around AI-assisted media asset management and video intelligence, and we want to prove which creator segment has the most urgent operational pain and budget.

The next milestone is not adding more features. It is validating a specific buyer, measuring the current workaround, testing a repeatable workflow and establishing a credible path to paid adoption. We would value ERA's perspective on the customer, wedge and evidence required to build a focused company.

### Suggested meeting agenda

1. Five minutes: founder, product and problem.
2. Five minutes: live workflow demo and what is genuinely working today.
3. Five minutes: customer segment and discovery evidence.
4. Five minutes: open questions on wedge, pricing, buyer and distribution.
5. Five minutes: program fit, application criteria and next step.

### Questions to ask ERA

- Which customer segment or vertical SaaS patterns are most relevant to the current cohort?
- What proof of customer urgency and willingness to pay is expected at this stage?
- Which parts of the current product story sound too broad or undifferentiated?
- What would make the initial wedge investable rather than merely interesting?
- Are there mentors or founders with experience in creator software, media operations or vertical SaaS?
- What is the current application timetable, eligibility and investment structure?

### What to attach

- One-page company brief.
- 3–5 minute product demo.
- One-page customer discovery summary, including negative findings.
- Current capability/status map.
- Founder bios and company facts.
- A one-page 90-day validation plan.

### Follow-up success criteria

A qualified next step is a clear eligibility answer, a named application route, specific feedback on the wedge, or a relevant introduction. A polite reply or event attendance is not a partnership milestone.

## 4. Partner brief: Forum Ventures

### Strategic angle

**Position ViewTube as a focused B2B SaaS workflow opportunity for creator teams, agencies or media operators — and prove who pays.**

This brief should not assume that a solo creator is the best buyer. Explore the operational buyer who coordinates multiple channels, people, assets, approvals and recurring production. If interviews show that the solo creator has the strongest pain and budget, update the pitch rather than forcing a B2B team narrative.

**Fit hypothesis:** Forum Ventures may be relevant if its current B2B SaaS focus, stage and program/investment criteria fit ViewTube's actual status. Verify the current model and application route.

### Tailored pitch

ViewTube is developing a connected operating workspace for video creators and teams. The problem we are investigating is operational fragmentation: research, production plans, media assets, handoffs, publishing tasks and performance learning are spread across disconnected tools, making it difficult to preserve context and repeat successful workflows.

Our commercial hypothesis is that creator agencies, production teams or professional media operators may pay for a unified workflow that improves asset reuse, coordination and evidence-backed production decisions. We are testing this against the alternative that professional solo creators are the stronger initial customer.

We want to build a focused B2B SaaS business around one high-frequency workflow, with clear buyer ownership, measurable value and a repeatable onboarding and sales motion.

### Best first ask

A focused B2B SaaS fit discussion or application review, including feedback on:
- Buyer selection: agency owner, operations lead, producer, channel manager or individual creator.
- Wedge selection and competitive differentiation.
- Pricing and packaging assumptions.
- Pilot design and conversion criteria.
- Founder-led sales and distribution.

### Two-minute meeting version

ViewTube connects the work that happens around professional video production: research, projects, assets, AI-assisted workflows, publishing operations and performance learning. We believe the fragmentation between these stages creates recurring operational cost, especially for teams managing multiple channels and deliverables.

Our key question is who feels that pain strongly enough to buy. We are testing agencies and creator teams against professional solo creators, and we want to narrow to one buyer and one workflow before scaling the product surface. We would value help pressure-testing the buyer, packaging the product as a repeatable SaaS offer and designing pilots that prove operational value.

### Buyer hypotheses to test

| Candidate buyer | Pain hypothesis | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Agency owner | Margin leakage and inconsistent client delivery | Budget ownership, workflow cost, willingness to pay |
| Head of production / producer | Handoffs, asset retrieval, schedule and version friction | Current tools, time loss, repeat frequency |
| Channel / content operations lead | Cross-channel coordination and reporting | Number of channels, users, approvals and workflows |
| Professional solo creator | Tool fragmentation and repetitive work | Usage frequency, switching threshold, price sensitivity |
| Publisher / media operations | Asset library, rights, metadata and scale | Archive requirements, security, procurement and integration needs |

### Pilot proposal to discuss

A bounded 4–6 week design-partner evaluation, only if a qualified partner is interested and the product is ready:
1. Select one team and one recurring workflow.
2. Record baseline process, tools, handoffs and pain points.
3. Configure only the existing capabilities needed for the workflow.
4. Track completion, retrieval, handoff and adoption events.
5. Review weekly with the partner.
6. Compare outcomes against the baseline and document limitations.
7. Decide whether to continue, pay, expand or stop.

No pilot should be described as available until a real partner, scope, access, data rights and delivery capacity are confirmed.

### Questions to ask Forum Ventures

- Which B2B buyer is most promising for a creator-operations product?
- What minimum evidence is expected for a repeatable SaaS pain?
- How should the company test agency/team versus solo-creator demand?
- What pilot design best demonstrates value without over-customization?
- What pricing signals or sales-cycle risks should be tested first?
- Which current program track and investment model, if any, fit the company's stage?

### What to attach

- One-page company brief with B2B buyer hypotheses.
- Product demo centered on one team workflow.
- Customer interview summary and disconfirming evidence.
- Pilot measurement plan.
- Current product-status and technical limitation sheet.
- Founder/company facts and fundraising status.

### Follow-up success criteria

A qualified next step is a relevant program route, specific B2B positioning feedback, a mentor conversation, or a credible design-partner introduction. Do not count an unqualified lead list as pipeline validation.

## 5. Partner brief: Google Cloud / Google for Startups

### Strategic angle

**Propose a bounded technical validation exercise with measurable workload, cost and data boundaries — not a generic cloud-credit request.**

The technical pitch must begin with the actual current architecture. Do not imply that Google Cloud, Vertex AI or Gemini is already in production unless the repository, deployment and billing evidence confirms it. A Google technology used in a local experiment is not proof of production deployment.

**Fit hypothesis:** Google Cloud or a current startup program may be relevant if the company meets the current eligibility rules and has a workload that benefits from technical review. Program names, benefits, credit amounts and access routes are all verify-before-sending items.

### Tailored pitch

ViewTube is building a connected workflow platform for professional video creators and teams. One technical challenge is supporting media-rich workflows and AI-assisted creator intelligence while keeping the system measurable, cost-aware and respectful of data rights and platform policies.

We would like to evaluate a small, clearly scoped technical workload rather than request infrastructure support on an unvalidated architecture. The first candidate is a single creator-media workflow with defined inputs, outputs, quality criteria, latency measurement, storage assumptions and cost-per-job reporting.

We are seeking technical guidance on architecture, model/service selection, cost controls and startup-program fit. Any Google Cloud or Gemini deployment would be treated as a proposed evaluation until it is implemented and verified.

### Best first ask

A technical office-hours or architecture-review conversation covering:
- Current startup-program eligibility and referral requirements.
- Architecture review for one bounded media/AI workload.
- Model and service selection, including provider-neutral alternatives.
- Cost estimation and measurement design.
- Data handling, consent, retention, security and YouTube/API policy boundaries.

### Proposed technical validation (not yet a commitment)

**Use case:** evaluate one media/creator-intelligence task with consented or otherwise authorized test material.

**Before the test:**
- Document the actual current architecture and existing providers.
- Define the task, representative dataset and permitted data use.
- Establish baseline quality, latency and cost measurement.
- Define privacy, retention and deletion requirements.
- Agree on success thresholds before running the benchmark.

**During the test:**
- Record model/service/version and configuration.
- Record input size, output, latency, errors and retries.
- Calculate cost using actual billing or clearly labeled rate assumptions.
- Compare with a baseline and at least one alternative where practical.
- Keep human review for subjective or high-impact outputs.

**After the test:**
- Publish an internal technical evaluation with limitations.
- Decide whether the provider is suitable for this workload.
- Do not generalize one task's result to all ViewTube workflows.
- Do not publish provider or performance claims without permission and reproducible evidence.

### Technical evidence checklist

| Item | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Architecture | Current system diagram with deployed vs local/proposed services distinguished |
| Workload | Task definition, data shape, volume assumptions and frequency |
| Data rights | Source, consent/authorization, YouTube API policy assessment and retention |
| Security | Identity, secrets, access control, isolation and deletion boundaries |
| Model evaluation | Quality rubric, test set, human review and failure categories |
| Performance | Reproducible latency, throughput and error methodology |
| Economics | Actual invoice or labeled pricing assumptions, cost per job and sensitivity |
| Operations | Monitoring, retries, rate limits, quotas and fallback plan |
| Company eligibility | Entity, stage, geography, founder and current program requirements |

### Questions to ask Google Cloud

- Which current startup program or technical support path fits this company's legal and funding stage?
- Is a referral required, and what evidence is expected?
- Which architecture-review resources are available for media and AI workloads?
- What are the current terms, limits and restrictions of any startup benefits?
- What security, privacy and data governance expectations apply to the proposed workload?
- Is there a technical mentor who can review a provider-neutral workload design?

### What to attach

- Provider-neutral architecture diagram.
- One-page bounded workload brief.
- Data-flow and rights/consent note.
- Demo and current product status.
- Cost model with assumptions separated from actual billing.
- Company eligibility facts.

### Follow-up success criteria

A qualified next step is a confirmed eligibility path, technical review, benchmark design session or named technical contact. Credits are not the primary success metric; verified workload fit and useful technical learning are.

## 6. Partner brief: Betaworks

### Strategic angle

**Lead with the product experience and a sharp experiment: can ViewTube make creator work feel meaningfully more connected?**

This is a product-thesis and experimentation pitch, not an infrastructure pitch. The most compelling story is a concrete before/after workflow: a creator moves from research to a production decision, finds or reuses an asset, coordinates the work and carries learning forward without losing context.

**Fit hypothesis:** Betaworks may be relevant if its current programs, investment themes, stage and product interests align with ViewTube's actual state. Verify current program focus and application routes before outreach.

### Tailored pitch

ViewTube is exploring a simple product idea: the creator's work should not lose its context every time it moves to another tool. Research, production plans, media assets, publishing tasks and performance learning are connected parts of one creative process, but most teams experience them as separate systems.

We are building a workspace that links those steps and are testing a focused entry point around media asset management and video intelligence. The product question is whether a context-rich workflow can make creators and teams faster at deciding what to make, finding what they already own and carrying useful learning into the next production cycle.

We want candid product feedback and a disciplined experiment: one creator segment, one end-to-end workflow, clear adoption signals and a short iteration loop.

### Best first ask

A product-focused conversation, demo critique or program-fit review:
- Is the workflow differentiated enough to matter?
- Which moment in the demo creates the clearest “aha”?
- What should be removed to make the product legible?
- Which experiment could validate repeat use and user pull?
- Is there a current program, community or founder network relevant to this product?

### Demo narrative: one creator workflow

1. **Start with a real problem:** a creator has research, ideas and references scattered across tools.
2. **Show context capture:** the creator preserves the source and rationale behind a content opportunity.
3. **Move into production:** the idea becomes a project or structured production plan.
4. **Use media context:** the creator locates or organizes relevant assets, with rights/provenance clearly represented where supported.
5. **Connect intelligence:** the creator reviews available evidence and distinguishes measured data from AI suggestions.
6. **Close the loop:** the creator records what was learned and what to test next.

Only show steps that work in the current build. If a step is planned or incomplete, say so and use a static roadmap slide rather than simulating a live capability.

### Product experiments to propose

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Context continuity test | Creators value preserving research and decisions across stages | Fewer lost references; successful task handoff |
| Asset retrieval test | Better asset context improves findability and reuse | Retrieval success, time-to-find, reuse events |
| Workflow activation test | One connected workflow is more compelling than a module tour | First-workflow completion and repeat use |
| AI trust test | Evidence-linked suggestions are more useful than generic generation | Acceptance, edits, rejection reasons and trust rating |
| Narrow-wedge test | One target segment has a stronger recurring problem | Interview evidence, repeat usage and willingness to pay |

These are experiment proposals. Do not report results until they have been run with documented methods and consent.

### Questions to ask Betaworks

- What part of the product thesis feels most differentiated?
- Is the connected-workflow idea clear, or does it read as an overly broad suite?
- Which demo moment should be the product's center of gravity?
- What would constitute meaningful user pull at this stage?
- Which current program or community is relevant to creator tools, AI applications or workflow software?
- What product or founder evidence would make a follow-up worthwhile?

### What to attach

- Short product demo.
- One-page product thesis and initial wedge.
- A single end-to-end workflow diagram.
- Experiment plan with measurable signals.
- Product-status map and known limitations.
- Founder/company facts.

### Follow-up success criteria

A qualified next step is a candid product review, a relevant program route, a specific experiment recommendation or a user/community introduction. General enthusiasm without a testable next action is not validation.

## 8. Shared 10-slide partner/investor deck outline

This is a deck narrative, not a claim that a presentation file or final investor deck has been created.

1. **Title / thesis** — ViewTube: connected workflows for professional video creators.
2. **The problem** — Show the fragmented creator workflow and the cost of context loss. Use interview evidence, not unsupported market statistics.
3. **The initial customer** — Identify the chosen segment and buyer. If unresolved, present the competing hypotheses honestly.
4. **The product** — One end-to-end live workflow, not a grid of every module.
5. **Why this is different** — Context continuity, asset lineage and evidence-linked decisions; support each claim with a product example or label it as a design goal.
6. **Market and alternatives** — Define the target use case and alternatives. Use freshly verified competitor sources and avoid unsourced TAM figures.
7. **Validation** — Customer interviews, usability findings, usage and pilot results. If none exist, show the learning plan and explicitly say evidence is early.
8. **Business model** — Pricing hypotheses and buyer economics. Label untested assumptions; do not fabricate revenue or conversion.
9. **Roadmap and risks** — Narrow milestones, technical dependencies, rights/privacy constraints and what is deliberately deferred.
10. **The ask** — One tailored request for the recipient, the reason they are a fit and the exact next step.

### Deck design rules

- One idea per slide; show the workflow visually.
- Prefer a real product screenshot or live demo over concept art.
- Label prototype, mockup and production UI accurately.
- Avoid logo walls, unsupported market-size numbers and inflated AI claims.
- Include a source/date footnote for every external market or competitor claim.
- Keep an appendix for architecture, security, data rights, metrics and detailed product status.
