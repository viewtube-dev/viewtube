## 1. Executive purpose

Create a repeatable partner-development system that helps ViewTube secure the most valuable next steps across:

1. Accelerator and investor access.
2. Cloud and AI infrastructure support.
3. Technical validation and developer ecosystem access.
4. Creator, agency, publisher and production-company pilots.

6. Distribution, co-marketing and channel partnerships.

The objective is not to collect logos or maximize headline credit amounts. It is to obtain the highest-value verified next step: customer discovery, a design partner, a pilot, technical review, eligible infrastructure support, a qualified investor conversation, or a signed commercial relationship.

## 2. Current ViewTube positioning for external use

### Approved working description

**ViewTube is a connected creator-workflow platform designed to bring video production, media assets, creator intelligence, analytics, AI-assisted workflows and publishing operations into one coherent workspace.**

The current proposed commercial wedge is **AI-assisted media asset management and video intelligence for professional creators and creator teams**. The broader platform thesis is connecting fragmented research, planning, production, asset, packaging, publishing and learning workflows.

This wedge is a strategic hypothesis, not a validated market conclusion. The initial buyer may be a professional solo creator, creator team, agency, publisher or media operation. Customer discovery must identify which has the most urgent problem and budget.

### Short pitch

ViewTube is building a connected workspace for professional video creators and teams. Instead of scattering research, production plans, media assets, content intelligence, publishing work and performance learning across disconnected tools, ViewTube aims to connect the workflow and preserve context between each stage. We are validating the strongest initial use case with creators and media teams.

### 30-second spoken pitch

Professional creators and media teams work across a fragmented stack: research in one place, scripts and plans in another, media assets somewhere else, editing and publishing elsewhere, and performance learning in separate analytics tools. ViewTube is building a connected creator workspace that links those steps through shared projects, media context, AI-assisted workflows and analytics. We are narrowing the first commercial wedge around media asset management and video intelligence, and we are looking for partners who can help validate the workflow with real creators and teams.

### Positioning exclusions

Do not pitch ViewTube as:
- An open-source privacy-first YouTube frontend.
- An ad blocker or alternative YouTube player.
- A proven retention-uplift or revenue-increase engine.
- A production-scale enterprise media archive unless scale is measured.
- A deployed Vertex/Gemini/Azure/AWS/NVIDIA architecture unless current code and deployment evidence confirm it.
- A current partner, customer, investor, accelerator member or endorsed product of any organization listed here.

## 3. Product capability and claim discipline

Every pitch must distinguish four states:

| State | Meaning | External wording |
|---|---|---|
| Implemented and verified | Current code and relevant tests/runtime evidence confirm behavior | “Available in the current product” only with a dated evidence reference |
| Implemented, verification incomplete | Source exists but behavior/build/runtime not fully verified | “In development / undergoing validation” |
| Planned or proposed | Roadmap, design or strategy only | “Planned”, “we are exploring”, or “proposed” |
| Partner-accelerated opportunity | Could be enabled or improved through a partner | “Potential collaboration” or “a use case we would like to evaluate” |

### Claims prohibited until evidenced

- Number of creators, customers, active users, paying accounts or pilots.
- Revenue, ARR, daily revenue, LTV, CAC, conversion, churn or retention.
- Hours or terabytes of video processed.
- Throughput, latency, accuracy, cost savings or reliability.
- Retention/drop-off prediction accuracy or audience revenue uplift.
- Customer ROI or percentage of editing/production time saved.
- Production use of a cloud, AI model, API, GPU or third-party provider.
- Current program eligibility, credit amounts, application windows or accepted status.
- Signed partnership, distribution, endorsement or case study.

For each quantitative statement, keep the metric definition, data source, sample, time window, baseline, calculation, limitations and owner. If evidence does not exist, use a clearly labeled hypothesis or omit the number.

## 7. Pitch package: documents and assets to prepare

### A. Master 12-slide deck

1. **Title and one-liner** — ViewTube and the focused creator workflow problem.
2. **Problem** — show the real fragmented workflow and the user cost; use interview evidence, not invented statistics.
3. **Initial customer** — define one buyer and one daily user; explain why this segment comes first.
4. **Solution** — demonstrate the connected workflow using real current product paths.
5. **Product walkthrough** — screenshots/video from a dated build; mark sample data and unfinished paths.
6. **Differentiation** — workflow continuity, shared project/asset context, evidence and handoffs; compare only with sourced competitor findings.
7. **Market** — bottom-up market model with explicit assumptions and sources; no unsupported TAM.
8. **Business model** — pricing hypothesis, buyer, packaging and cost drivers; label untested assumptions.
9. **Technology and trust** — actual architecture, permissions, data lineage, privacy, provider dependencies and YouTube API constraints.
10. **Validation and traction** — interviews, pilots, usage and outcomes only where documented; otherwise show the learning plan.
11. **Roadmap and team** — verified capabilities, next milestones, founder-market fit and capability gaps.
12. **Partner-specific ask** — why this partner, mutual value, exact request and the next decision.

### B. Partner-specific one-page brief

Every partner one-pager should contain:
- One-sentence ViewTube description.
- Partner-specific problem/strategic fit.
- One relevant workflow demo.
- Verified current capability vs planned opportunity.
- Partner value and proposed collaboration.
- Exact ask, duration and success metric.
- Evidence and open questions.
- Source URLs, access dates and terms requiring confirmation.

### C. Two-minute demo

1. Start with a recognizable creator or team task.
2. Show the current fragmented process and the point of friction.
3. Demonstrate the corresponding ViewTube workflow using actual working features.
4. Show how context, project, asset or evidence is handed to the next step.
5. Identify sample data, unavailable functions and planned capabilities.
6. End with the specific pilot question.

Do not show mockups as if they were production functionality. Avoid fabricated analytics curves or performance improvements.

### D. Data-room structure

- 00 — Company overview and founder bios.
- 01 — Pitch deck and one-pager.
- 02 — Product demo and dated release notes.
- 03 — Capability matrix with source/test references.
- 04 — Architecture, security, privacy and data-flow overview.
- 05 — Customer discovery and interview synthesis.
- 06 — Pilot scopes, agreements and results.
- 07 — Market and competitor evidence.
- 08 — Financial model, pricing assumptions and infrastructure cost model.
- 09 — IP ownership, contributor agreements and open-source license review.
- 10 — YouTube API policy/compliance and data-retention review.
- 11 — Partner-specific applications and correspondence.
- 12 — Fundraising materials, cap table and company formation documents (share only with appropriate recipients).

Never place secrets, API keys, private creator data, OAuth tokens or unredacted customer records in a general partner data room.

## 8. Outreach planning and templates

### Outreach sequence

1. Identify the exact partner team, program or person from an official source.
2. Verify current program/role, geography, eligibility, application window and contact route.
3. Find a legitimate warm introduction where available; record provenance.
4. Send a short tailored note with one relevant demo/one-pager and one ask.
5. Follow up once after 5–7 business days with new information or a useful clarification.
6. If no response, move to a lower-cost nurture path; do not spam multiple staff.
7. Record outcome, next action, date and evidence in the partner tracker.

## 9. Objection handling and evidence responses

| Likely objection | Response approach | Evidence to show / next step |
|---|---|---|
| “This is too broad.” | Agree that the long-term platform is broad; lead with one narrow buyer and workflow. | Focused use case, interview evidence, 2-minute demo |
| “Who pays?” | Do not guess. Explain buyer hypotheses and discovery status. | Buyer interviews, budget owner, pricing tests |
| “Why not use existing tools?” | Show the handoff gap and workflow continuity, not a feature checklist. | Sourced competitor matrix and task walkthrough |
| “Is this live?” | Separate verified, incomplete, planned and partner-enabled capabilities. | Dated build, code/test references, demo |
| “Do you have traction?” | Give actual numbers only if documented; otherwise say validation is in progress. | Cohort definition, source query, period and calculation |
| “Does it predict retention?” | Do not claim predictive accuracy without a validated model and evaluation. | Model spec, labeled dataset, baseline, held-out results |
| “How do you access YouTube data?” | Explain authorized scopes and policy constraints; do not imply access to private data without consent. | OAuth flow, policy review, deletion and revocation behavior |
| “What does it cost to run?” | Present measured cost per representative workflow or a transparent estimate with assumptions. | Cloud bills, workload size, service rates and sensitivity model |
| “Why this partner?” | Tie the ask to a specific capability, market access or program. | Partner-specific fit brief and mutual-value case |
| “What do you need?” | Ask for one next step, not a vague partnership. | Eligibility review, pilot, intro, mentor session or technical review |

## 13. Commercial, legal and data safeguards

Before any partnership, referral, revenue share, pilot, integration or research agreement:
- Define scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, timeline and termination.
- Define data controller/processor roles, permitted use, access, retention and deletion.
- Confirm YouTube API and third-party license restrictions; obtain current legal review.
- Define IP ownership for pre-existing and newly created work.
- Define confidentiality and publication approval.
- Set security, incident notification and subcontractor requirements.
- Avoid broad exclusivity, perpetual revenue share, rights to all future IP or unrestricted customer-data access.
- Require attribution rules, refund/chargeback handling, payment schedule, audit and termination for any performance-based fee.
- Do not offer equity or a 15–20% net-profit share as a casual outreach inducement; these are unapproved historical proposals requiring financial/legal review.
- Use signed pilot agreements and explicit participant consent for creator media and analytics data.
