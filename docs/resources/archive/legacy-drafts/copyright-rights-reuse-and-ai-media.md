# Copyright, Rights, Reuse & AI Media

## Purpose

Creators need a rights-aware workflow for original work, licensed material, reused content, user submissions, music, footage, images, and AI-generated or AI-assisted media.

## Rights are contextual

Credit, transformation, commentary, education, or noncommercial use does not automatically make a use lawful. Copyright exceptions and platform policies vary by jurisdiction and situation.

## Before publishing

For every non-original asset, record source, rights holder, license or permission, allowed uses, attribution requirements, geographic or time limits, proof of permission, and restrictions on modification or commercial use.

## Reuse

Reusing your own material can be efficient, but distinguish it from material for which you do not control rights. Platform monetization and copyright enforcement can apply separate standards.

## AI-assisted media

Document AI use when provenance matters. Verify generated or transformed material for factual errors, unwanted likenesses, copyrighted inputs, privacy issues, and misleading representations.

## Claims and likeness

Do not assume that synthetic imitation of a person, voice, brand, or protected work is safe merely because it is AI-generated. Consider applicable law, platform rules, permissions, and disclosure requirements.

## Operational policy

When rights are uncertain, pause publication and escalate for appropriate legal review rather than relying on an informal rule of thumb.

## Principle

Treat provenance as metadata. A creator should be able to answer where an asset came from, what permission exists, and why the planned use is allowed.
